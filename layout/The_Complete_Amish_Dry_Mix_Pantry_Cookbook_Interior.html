All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix

This is the mix you will reach for most often. With the fat already cut into the flour, a batch of biscuits is only a stir and a few minutes in the oven away.

Makesabout 10 cups
Prep15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 8 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 1/3 cups vegetable shortening

How to Use

For 9 drop biscuits: stir 2 1/4 cups mix with 2/3 cup milk until a soft dough forms. Drop onto a baking sheet and bake at 450°F for 8 to 10 minutes.

To measure, spoon the mix lightly into the cup and level it off. Never scoop straight from the jar, or the biscuits will be dry.

Directions

  1. In a very large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, sugar and salt until evenly combined.
  2. Add the shortening in spoonfuls. Cut it in with a pastry blender, two forks or your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs with no pieces larger than a small pea.
  3. Spoon the mix into clean, dry airtight jars or containers without packing it down. Label with the name and date.
KeepsPantry up to 6 weeks; refrigerator up to 3 months; freezer up to 6 months

Pantry Tip

Shortening keeps longer on the shelf than butter. If you prefer an all-butter mix, store it in the refrigerator and use it within 1 month.

Buttermilk Biscuit Mix

Powdered buttermilk gives these biscuits their old-fashioned tang without a fresh carton in the fridge. The fat is added on baking day, so the mix keeps for months.

Makesabout 8 cups (3 batches)
Prep10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 6 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup buttermilk powder
  • 3 tablespoons baking powder
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking soda
  • 1 tablespoon granulated sugar
  • 2 teaspoons salt

How to Use

For 8 biscuits: place 2 1/2 cups mix in a bowl and cut in 6 tablespoons cold butter until crumbly. Stir in 3/4 cup cold water just until the dough comes together. Pat out, cut and bake at 425°F for 12 to 15 minutes.

Directions

  1. Whisk all the ingredients together in a large bowl until the buttermilk powder is evenly distributed and there are no lumps.
  2. Transfer to an airtight container and label.
KeepsPantry up to 3 months; refrigerator up to 6 months

Pantry Tip

Buttermilk powder clumps when it meets humidity. Shake the jar well before each use.

Whole Wheat Baking Mix

A heartier version of the all-purpose mix, made with half whole wheat flour for a nutty flavor and a little more fiber.

Makesabout 10 cups
Prep15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 4 cups whole wheat flour
  • 4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons packed brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 1/3 cups vegetable shortening

How to Use

Use it in place of All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix in any recipe in this chapter. Add 1 to 2 tablespoons extra liquid, because whole wheat flour absorbs more.

Directions

  1. Whisk the flours, baking powder, brown sugar and salt in a very large bowl, breaking up any lumps of sugar.
  2. Cut in the shortening until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs.
  3. Store in airtight containers and label.
KeepsPantry up to 1 month; refrigerator up to 3 months; freezer up to 6 months

Pantry Tip

Whole wheat flour contains natural oils that can turn stale, so this mix is best kept cold.

Homemade Self-Rising Flour Blend

Many old family recipes call for self-rising flour. Mix up your own and you will never be caught without it.

Makesabout 10 cups
Prep5 minutes

Ingredients

  • 10 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/3 cup baking powder
  • 2 1/2 teaspoons salt

How to Use

Use cup for cup in any recipe that calls for self-rising flour, and leave out any extra baking powder and salt the recipe lists.

Directions

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder and salt together very thoroughly, at least 1 full minute, so the leavening is evenly spread.
  2. Store in an airtight container and label.
KeepsPantry up to 6 months

Pantry Tip

Baking powder slowly loses strength. Mark the date on the lid and use the blend within 6 months.

Fluffy Amish Drop Biscuits

No rolling and no cutting: stir, drop and bake. These are the everyday biscuits of a busy farm kitchen.

Makes9 biscuits
Prep5 minutes
Cook10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 2 1/4 cups All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix
  • 2/3 cup milk
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter, for brushing (optional)

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 450°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper or grease it lightly.
  2. Stir the baking mix and milk with a fork just until a soft dough forms. Do not overmix.
  3. Drop the dough by heaping spoonfuls 2 inches apart onto the baking sheet.
  4. Bake for 8 to 10 minutes, until golden on top. Brush with melted butter, if you like, and serve warm.

Flaky Buttermilk Cut Biscuits

Tall, tender and layered, with a buttery crumb and a gentle buttermilk tang.

Makes8 biscuits
Prep15 minutes
Cook15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups Buttermilk Biscuit Mix
  • 6 tablespoons cold butter, cut into small cubes
  • 3/4 cup cold water

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. Place the biscuit mix in a bowl. Cut in the cold butter until the pieces are the size of small peas.
  3. Stir in the cold water just until the dough holds together.
  4. Turn the dough onto a floured surface and pat it into a rectangle about 1 inch thick. Fold it in half, pat it out again, and repeat twice more to build flaky layers.
  5. Cut straight down with a 2 1/2-inch round cutter, without twisting. Place the biscuits close together on the baking sheet.
  6. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until tall and golden brown.

Pantry Tip

Twisting the cutter seals the edges and keeps the biscuits from rising. Press straight down and lift straight up.

Biscuits and Country Sausage Gravy

A hearty farmhouse breakfast of warm biscuits smothered in peppery sausage gravy.

Serves4 to 6
Prep10 minutes
Cook20 minutes

Ingredients

For the biscuits
  • 1 batch Fluffy Amish Drop Biscuits
For the gravy
  • 1 pound bulk pork breakfast sausage
  • 1/3 cup All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix
  • 2 1/2 cups milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper, plus more to taste
  • Salt, to taste

Directions

  1. Bake the drop biscuits and keep them warm.
  2. Cook the sausage in a large skillet over medium heat, breaking it into small crumbles, until no longer pink, 8 to 10 minutes. Do not drain.
  3. Sprinkle the baking mix over the sausage and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Slowly pour in the milk, stirring constantly. Cook and stir until the gravy bubbles and thickens, 4 to 5 minutes.
  5. Season with pepper and salt. Split the biscuits and spoon the gravy over the top.

Pantry Tip

If the gravy gets too thick as it stands, stir in a splash of milk.

Old-Fashioned Chicken and Dumplings

A soul-warming pot of tender chicken, vegetables and rich broth topped with pillowy dumplings.

Serves6
Prep15 minutes
Cook35 minutes

Ingredients

For the stew
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, sliced
  • 2 celery stalks, sliced
  • 8 cups chicken broth
  • 3 cups cooked chicken, shredded or diced
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
For the dumplings
  • 2 cups Drop Dumpling Mix
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 3/4 cup milk

Directions

  1. Melt the butter in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots and celery and cook until softened, about 6 minutes.
  2. Add the broth and thyme. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer for 10 minutes, until the carrots are tender.
  3. Stir in the chicken and season with salt and pepper. Keep the stew at a gentle simmer.
  4. In a bowl, stir the dumpling mix, melted butter and milk just until moistened.
  5. Drop the dough by heaping tablespoons onto the simmering stew. Cover tightly and cook for 15 minutes without lifting the lid.
  6. The dumplings are done when they are puffed and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. Serve at once.

Easy Crustless Ham and Cheese Pie

The baking mix settles to form its own tender crust as the pie bakes. Perfect for brunch or a light supper.

Serves6
Prep10 minutes
Cook35 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 cup diced cooked ham
  • 1 cup shredded cheddar cheese
  • 1/2 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1/2 cup All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix
  • 1 cup milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F and grease a 9-inch pie plate.
  2. Scatter the ham, cheese and onion evenly in the pie plate.
  3. Whisk the baking mix, milk, eggs and pepper until smooth. Pour over the ham and cheese.
  4. Bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until golden and a knife inserted in the center comes out clean.
  5. Let stand for 5 minutes before slicing.

Pantry Tip

Swap the ham for cooked sausage, bacon or a cup of steamed broccoli.

Chicken Pot Pie with Biscuit Topping

All the comfort of a classic pot pie, with a golden biscuit topping instead of a fussy rolled crust.

Serves6
Prep20 minutes
Cook30 minutes

Ingredients

For the filling
  • 1/4 cup butter
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1/3 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 3/4 cups chicken broth
  • 2/3 cup milk
  • 3 cups cooked chicken, diced
  • 2 cups frozen mixed vegetables
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
For the topping
  • 1 1/2 cups All-Purpose Amish Baking Mix
  • 1/2 cup milk

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F.
  2. Melt the butter in a large oven-safe skillet or saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute. Gradually whisk in the broth and milk. Cook, stirring, until thick and bubbly.
  4. Stir in the chicken, vegetables, thyme, salt and pepper. If your skillet is not oven-safe, pour the filling into a 2-quart baking dish.
  5. Stir the baking mix and milk until a soft dough forms. Drop by spoonfuls over the hot filling.
  6. Bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until the topping is golden and the filling is bubbling at the edges.

Maple Brown Sugar Instant Oatmeal Mix

Skip the expensive single-serve packets. This homemade version is ready in two minutes and you choose the sweetness.

Makesabout 10 cups (20 servings)
Prep10 minutes

Ingredients

  • 8 cups quick-cooking oats
  • 1 cup nonfat dry milk powder
  • 1 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup maple sugar (or additional brown sugar)
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon salt

How to Use

For 1 serving: stir 1/2 cup mix with 2/3 cup boiling water. Cover and let stand for 2 minutes. Or microwave with 2/3 cup cold water for 1 to 1 1/2 minutes, stirring once.

  3. Store in an airtight container, or portion 1/2 cup into small bags for grab-and-go breakfasts.

Golden, tender pancakes in the time it takes to heat the griddle.

Makes10 to 12 pancakes
Prep5 minutes
Cook15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups Old-Fashioned Buttermilk Pancake Mix
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil or melted butter
  • Butter and maple syrup, to serve

Directions

  1. Heat a griddle or large skillet over medium heat and grease it lightly.
  2. Whisk the egg, water and oil in a bowl. Add the pancake mix and stir just until combined. Let the batter rest for 2 minutes.

Pantry Tip

Overmixing makes tough pancakes. Stop stirring as soon as no dry flour remains.

Pancake and sausage baked together in a muffin cup: a handheld breakfast for busy mornings.

Makes12 muffins
Prep15 minutes
Cook18 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1/2 pound bulk breakfast sausage, cooked, crumbled and drained
  • 1 1/2 cups Old-Fashioned Buttermilk Pancake Mix
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons maple syrup, plus more to serve
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F and grease a 12-cup muffin pan.
  2. Whisk the egg, water, syrup and butter. Stir in the pancake mix, then fold in the sausage.
  3. Divide the batter among the muffin cups.
  4. Bake for 15 to 18 minutes, until golden and a toothpick comes out clean.
  5. Serve warm with syrup for dipping.

Cook22 minutes

Ingredients

Cornbread-Topped Chili Bake

Hearty beef and bean chili baked under a golden cornbread crust: a complete one-pan supper.

Serves6
Prep20 minutes
Cook25 minutes

Ingredients

For the chili
  • 1 pound ground beef
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 can (15 ounces) kidney or pinto beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (14.5 ounces) diced tomatoes
  • 1 can (8 ounces) tomato sauce
  • 2 tablespoons chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
For the topping
  • 2 1/2 cups Amish Cornbread Mix
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F.
  2. In a 10- or 12-inch oven-safe skillet, cook the beef and onion over medium-high heat until browned. Drain the fat.
  3. Stir in the beans, tomatoes, tomato sauce, chili powder, cumin and salt. Simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Stir together the cornbread mix, egg, water and oil. Spoon the batter evenly over the hot chili.
  5. Bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until the topping is golden and a toothpick inserted in it comes out clean.

Amish Cinnamon Bread

Two sweet loaves with a crackly cinnamon-sugar crust. Keep one and share the other with a neighbor.

Makes2 loaves
Prep15 minutes
Cook55 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 bag Amish Cinnamon Bread Mix, with its cinnamon-sugar packet
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 cup buttermilk (or 1 cup milk mixed with 1 tablespoon lemon juice)
  • 2/3 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 325°F. Grease two 8 x 4-inch loaf pans.
  2. Sprinkle about half of the cinnamon-sugar into the pans and tilt to coat the bottoms and sides.
  3. Whisk the eggs, buttermilk, oil and vanilla. Stir in the bread mix until just combined.
  4. Divide the batter between the pans and sprinkle the remaining cinnamon-sugar over the tops.
  5. Bake for 50 to 60 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean.
  6. Cool in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack.
